For centuries the Jews of Ethiopia prayed to return to Jerusalem. In the 1970s, the dream began to come true.

## Recognition

In **1973**, the Sephardic Chief Rabbi, **Rabbi Ovadia Yosef**, ruled that Beta Israel are Jews. In **1975**, the Israeli government decided that the Law of Return applied to them, opening the way to aliyah (immigration to Israel).

## The journey through Sudan

In the late 1970s and early 1980s, thousands of community members left their villages in Ethiopia on foot for Sudan. The journey took long weeks, walking through deserts and mountains, in danger from bandits, hunger and disease. Many waited for months in refugee camps under very harsh conditions.

**About 4,000 members of the community died on the way to Jerusalem.** A memorial in their honor stands on Mount Herzl, and the official memorial day is marked on Jerusalem Day, the 28th of Iyar.

## Operation Moses (1984–1985)

In a secret operation, thousands of immigrants were brought to Israel from Sudan. When the operation was exposed in the media it was halted, and many families were left divided: some in Israel and some still in Ethiopia.

## Operation Solomon (1991)

In May 1991, while Addis Ababa was under siege, **more than 14,000 immigrants were flown to Israel in about a day and a half**. It was one of the largest and most moving rescue operations in the country's history.

## Today

Today more than 160,000 members of the Ethiopian community live in Israel, many of them born in the country. The community contributes to every area of Israeli life while working to preserve the traditions, language and culture it brought with it. The synagogue in Neve Yaakov is part of that effort.
