**Sigd** is the holiday unique to the Beta Israel community. It falls on the **29th of Cheshvan**, fifty days after Yom Kippur. The name "Sigd" comes from a Ge'ez root meaning **bowing down**.

## The meaning of the holiday

Sigd celebrates the **renewal of the covenant** between the people of Israel and God, like the covenant renewed in the days of Ezra and Nehemiah. It is a day of fasting, prayer, self-reflection and asking forgiveness, and at its heart is **the longing for Jerusalem** and the Temple.

## Sigd in Ethiopia

In Ethiopia, community members dressed in white climbed a high mountain near the village. There the Kessim read from the Orit and led the prayers. At the end of the day everyone came down the mountain singing and dancing, and broke the fast with a shared meal.

## Sigd in Jerusalem

Since the community's aliyah to Israel, the main Sigd ceremony has been held in Jerusalem, on the **Armon HaNatziv promenade** overlooking the Old City. The dream of generations came true: the longing for Jerusalem became a prayer inside Jerusalem.

In 2008, Sigd was established by law as a **national holiday** of the State of Israel, and today many people outside the community take part as well.
