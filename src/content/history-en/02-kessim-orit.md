## The Kessim

The **Kes** (plural: **Kessim**) is the spiritual and religious leader of the community. The Kessim lead prayers and holiday ceremonies, preserve the tradition, teach the younger generation, and mediate and advise on family and community matters.

The training is long: a young Kes studies for many years with senior Kessim, memorizing prayers and texts in Ge'ez and learning the laws of the tradition. Today in Israel, the Kessim still hold a place of honor in the community and its ceremonies.

## The Orit: the Torah in Ge'ez

The holy book of Beta Israel is the **Orit**. It contains the Five Books of Moses together with the books of Joshua, Judges and Ruth, and is written in **Ge'ez**, the ancient language of Ethiopia. The community's religious law rests mainly on the Written Torah. The Talmud and the Oral Torah developed in other Jewish communities, and reached Beta Israel only in modern times.

During prayers the Kes reads from the Orit in Ge'ez, and sometimes translates and explains it to the congregation in Amharic or Tigrinya.

## The Masgid: the house of prayer

The traditional house of prayer is called the **Masgid** ("place of bowing down"). In the villages of Ethiopia it was usually a simple building, and prayer, holidays and community gatherings were at the center of village life.

The community's synagogue in Neve Yaakov carries this tradition forward in the heart of Jerusalem: a place to pray according to the Beta Israel rite, celebrate the holidays, and pass the tradition on to future generations.
